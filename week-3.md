# Week 3 – Gospel Connection: Transformation Journal

*To my future children and descendants,*

I want to share a lesson from my journey as a software engineering student in the United States. Studying in another country, learning technical subjects in English, and working on difficult software projects have challenged me in ways I did not fully expect. There have been times when I felt overwhelmed by assignments, programming errors, deadlines, and uncertainty about whether I could finish everything on time. I have also had to plan carefully for graduation because some required courses are not offered when I need them.

I want you to know that these challenges did not disappear simply because I worked hard. Sometimes a solution required many attempts, asking for help, or changing my plan. I used to think that being capable meant finding answers quickly. Through these experiences, I have been learning that real strength also means being patient, admitting what I do not know, and continuing to improve.

This reminds me of the Extract, Transform, and Load (ETL) process we study in data engineering. In the **Extract** stage, data is collected in its original form, including missing values, inconsistencies, and errors. My own starting point was similar: I had goals and abilities, but also fears, weaknesses, and gaps in my knowledge. In the **Transform** stage, data is cleaned, checked, and reshaped for a useful purpose. For me, transformation has come through studying, debugging, accepting feedback, practicing humility, and choosing to try again. In the **Load** stage, prepared data becomes useful in a system. Likewise, the skills and character I develop can become useful when I support my family, collaborate with classmates, and build software that serves other people.

For example, when working with my team on our Steady Balance healthcare software project, I have learned that my code is not the only thing that matters. I need to communicate clearly, consider how my changes affect others, test my work, and help the team move toward a shared goal. Patience and responsibility can bless teammates as much as technical knowledge does. I hope these habits will also help me be a more dependable member of my future family.

In Isaiah 49:2, the servant is described as a polished shaft, prepared and kept for a purpose. I understand this image as a reminder that preparation can take time and that becoming useful to the Lord involves refinement. President Henry B. Eyring's message in *The Reward of Enduring Well* encourages me to see trials not only as obstacles, but also as opportunities to become more faithful and better prepared to serve. I do not believe every difficulty is pleasant or that I will always understand its purpose immediately. But I can choose how I respond to it.

During difficult moments, I want to choose faith by praying for guidance, doing the work in front of me, seeking help when necessary, and refusing to let one failure define my future. Faith does not replace studying or solving problems. It gives me a reason to keep going when progress is slow. Just as a program becomes more reliable through careful testing and correction, I hope my testimony will become stronger through repeated choices to trust Jesus Christ and live His teachings.

This experience is preparing me to become more resilient, humble, and compassionate. When someone else struggles with a difficult task, I want to remember how much I needed patience and encouragement. I want to help rather than judge. My trials matter because they are teaching me that success is not only about reaching graduation or becoming a skilled engineer. It is also about the kind of person I become along the way.

To my posterity, I hope you will remember this: do not be ashamed of starting with imperfect knowledge or facing problems you cannot solve immediately. Ask questions, seek truth, accept correction, and keep your faith in Jesus Christ. You may not control every trial, but you can choose to let your experiences refine your character. A stronger disciple is not someone who never struggles; it is someone who keeps turning toward the Savior and becomes more willing to serve.

I am still in the middle of my own transformation. I have not mastered every skill or overcome every weakness. But I hope that, through the grace of Jesus Christ and consistent effort, I can become a more durable defender of His gospel—someone who remains faithful under pressure, treats others with love, and uses what he learns to do good.

*With love and hope for your future,*  
*Your ancestor*
